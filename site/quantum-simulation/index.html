    Why Quantum Simulation?

    First-principles simulation provides a computational route for studying materials from their atomic and electronic description.

    Instead of treating a material only through experimentally measured properties, the computational workflow starts from its structure and chemical composition and develops a model for its electronic behavior.

    • Atomic Structure

      Define the chemical composition, atomic positions, crystal structure, and simulation cell.

    • Electronic Structure

      Describe the electronic state of the material using a quantum-mechanical framework.

    • First Principles

      Calculate material properties from fundamental physical principles rather than relying only on empirical fitting.

    Build the Computational Model

    Before running Quantum ESPRESSO, the physical material must be translated into a well-defined computational representation.

    • Crystal Structure

      Define the unit cell, lattice parameters, and atomic positions.

    • Chemical Species

      Specify the elements and their corresponding pseudopotentials.

    • Basis Settings

      Define the numerical representation used to describe the electronic wavefunctions.

    • k-Point Sampling

      Represent the periodicity of the electronic structure in reciprocal space.

    Calculation Path

    A single DFT calculation does not necessarily answer every electronic structure question.

    Different calculation stages serve different purposes.

    • SCF

      Establish the self-consistent electronic ground state.

      Purpose: Ground-state electronic density and total energy.

    • NSCF

      Perform a follow-up electronic calculation using a specified k-point sampling.

      Purpose: Prepare electronic information for further analysis.

    • Band Structure

      Calculate electronic energy levels along selected paths in reciprocal space.

      Purpose: Examine electronic dispersion.

    • Density of States

      Determine how electronic states are distributed as a function of energy.

      Purpose: Analyze the available electronic states.

    From Calculation to Insight

    A successful calculation is not the final research result.

    The numerical output must be checked, visualized, and interpreted.

    • Validate

      Check convergence and confirm that the calculation reached the intended numerical condition.

    • Extract

      Identify the physical quantities required by the research question.

    • Visualize

      Convert numerical results into interpretable plots and electronic-structure representations.

    • Interpret

      Connect computational observations with the physical behavior of the material.
